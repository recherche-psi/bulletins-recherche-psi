This issue examines a new synthesis of tests of post-mortem communication, a metascience study of AI-assisted citation checking and a philosophical analysis of conviction. The selected publications date from 19 to 22 October. No new controlled experimental psi finding was identified among the documents verified for this week. The main feature concerns test design and the conclusions that findings warrant.

## Scientific parapsychology

### Testing post-mortem communication using information prepared in advance

Willin and Matlock [1] • Created 21 October, updated 22 October 2026 • Historical and methodological synthesis in the Psi Encyclopedia • Editorial reference entry, distinct from a peer-reviewed journal article

[Full text](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/envelope-cipher-and-lock-tests-of-survival/)

A prospective test prepares its criterion before the event being studied. In the arrangements presented here, a person retains a sealed message, a decryption key or a secret combination during their lifetime. After their death, a medium must supply information enabling the alleged communication to be checked. The aim is to have a predetermined answer, rather than retrospectively seek similarities between an account and a biography.

The new contribution brings these test families together in a single entry, with historical references and directions to archives. It presents no new data collection, preregistered experiment or meta-analysis. It calculates neither an aggregate experimental sample size nor a common effect size.

| Arrangement | Historical assessment reported in the entry |
| --- | --- |
| Sealed message | Proposed matches provide no unambiguous success. |
| Thouless cipher text | Solutions were recovered by cryptanalysis, without post-mortem communication. |
| Combination lock | No correct and unambiguous communication of the combination is established. |

### What a successful outcome actually measures

The authors emphasise that accurate information would not automatically distinguish survival from psi among the living. Their examples make a central difficulty explicit: defining a verifiable observation and identifying its cause are two stages of inference [1].

Our analysis. Researchers must check who knows the answer and can access the document, then reconstruct its chain of custody. Even if ordinary access is excluded, what prediction distinguishes communication attributed to the deceased from another anomalous acquisition of information? An accurate answer would leave this causal question open.

Matching rules must be fixed in advance: an exact code and a semantic approximation do not have the same probability of coincidental occurrence. All responses must be retained before checking, and failed attempts counted, including those involving multiple people or sessions. An isolated success is difficult to assess when the full set of attempts is unknown.

Failure constrains the hypothesis of transmission under specified conditions, without refuting every conception of survival. A theory must specify these conditions and observations that would count against it. Invoking communication difficulties after each failure cannot replace an advance prediction.

The entry's strength is to bring together arrangements whose success appears easy to define. Its limitation is that of a historical selection: it provides no systematic inventory of attempts from which to estimate a success rate. The reported assessment should therefore become neither a numerical frequency nor a general demonstration of impossibility.

*Priority full reading, particularly the sections on ciphers, locks and the interpretation of tests in 2026.*

## Metascience

### Does a reference exist and does it actually support the text

Li et al. [2] • 19 October 2026 • Peer-reviewed cross-sectional study • Published version of work first released as a preprint on 30 June

[Full text](https://doi.org/10.2196/105947)

Metascience examines how knowledge is produced and checked. Here, an AI system gathers references and summaries for an English-Chinese anaesthesiology library. A separate AI-assisted procedure and human specialists then evaluate these citations. The question goes beyond finding an article: reviewers must check whether it addresses the topic, whether the summary respects its findings and whether the translation preserves their meaning.

The study compares 570 citations across 19 subspecialties, with 10 human validators. It was not preregistered; the binary grouping of judgments was post hoc. Human judgment serves as a comparison reference, without constituting independent truth. Intervals account for clustering by subspecialty [2].

| AI-human agreement measure | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Two categories after grouping | 489/570; 85.8%; 95% CI [79.12, 91.75] |
| Three categories retained | 271/570; 47.5%; 95% CI [41.93, 53.16] |
| Cohen's kappa for two categories | 0.0645; 95% CI [−0.0103, 0.1639] |

Combining “pass” and “conditional” verdicts therefore hides some disagreements. Cohen's kappa compares observed agreement with agreement expected from category frequencies. Its interpretation depends on these frequencies: high raw agreement alone cannot characterise concordance. Validator and subspecialty effects cannot be separated. Structured data are reported as public; they cannot reproduce the model's original retrieval runs [2].

Our analysis. This comparison evaluates agreement between judges, rather than the AI system's absolute accuracy. If both judges make the same error, agreement increases without improved validity. A complementary evaluation should have independent specialists assess disagreements without revealing the origin of the verdicts. It should specify error severity and the cost of wrongly discarded references in advance.

For a scientific synthesis, verification must address the link between each claim and its source. A correct reference can be misinterpreted or support a weaker conclusion than the summary asserts. Retaining the relevant passage, study conditions and justification for the interpretation makes this link inspectable. This applies equally to a psi review and a clinical resource.

*Priority full reading for the protocol, agreement tables and distinction between agreement and accuracy. Technical appendices support examination of the verification process.*

## Anomalistics and epistemology

### Understanding conviction without confusing experienced certainty with evidence

Nie [3] • 22 October 2026 • Peer-reviewed theoretical article • Philosophy of psychopathology

[Full text](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10670-026-01166-0)

An experience can create an immediate impression of knowing a fact, before any argument is considered. Chenwei Nie examines this phenomenon in delusional beliefs: how can someone recognise objections yet remain convinced about a specific point while reasoning normally about other matters? The article addresses this clinical and philosophical question; it does not test psi.

The author proposes that some experiences impose their content with exceptional subjective clarity. He distinguishes a causal inclination towards belief, a feeling of justification, a feeling of knowing and a persistent conflict between experience and contrary evidence. The work rests on conceptual analysis and existing literature. It supplies no new clinical sample, experimental finding or effect size [3].

Its contribution is to separate psychological functions often grouped under “conviction”. Its limitation is that these distinctions still need measures capable of discriminating between explanations. In particular, researchers would need to establish whether assessors can identify these dimensions separately within the same account.

Our analysis. For anomalistics, this separation can help describe an account without immediately deciding its cause. Felt certainty is evidence about experience; correspondence with a target or event must be checked separately. The article does not warrant equating reported psi experiences with delusion. Such an extension would require comparisons and clinical criteria absent from this work.

*Targeted reading: sections 4.2 and 4.3 for the conceptual distinctions, followed by 4.4 for their relationship to competing theories.*

## Synthesis and reading priorities

This week's additions concern the precision of verification criteria. The synthesis of post-mortem tests requires a correct answer to be separated from identification of its cause. The citation study shows how the definition of agreement changes what an evaluation measures. The analysis of conviction distinguishes experiential properties from justification of a claim. These contributions provide tools for designing and interpreting tests; they supply no new experimental finding on psi information acquisition.

1. Willin and Matlock [1]: full reading for the design and interpretation of prospective tests.

2. Li et al. [2]: full reading for citation verification and the limitations of agreement measures.

3. Nie [3]: sections 4.2 to 4.4 for analysis of convictions and the limits of its application to anomalistics.
