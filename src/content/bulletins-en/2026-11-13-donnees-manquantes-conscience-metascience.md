How can exclusions generate an apparently anomalous result? What would need to be measured to connect an unusual state of consciousness with psi performance? How should institutions promising more reliable science be evaluated? This issue addresses these three questions. It includes a methods entry updated on 8 November and two explicitly dated catch-up items: an article published on 6 November and a report deposited on 3 November, included in a grouped release on 12 November.

## Psi research: dropout and the selection of data

Kennedy [1] · Updated 8 November 2026 · Encyclopaedia methods entry; not a new peer-reviewed experimental article

In a forced-choice psi test, a person might try to guess which of two random targets will be presented. The null hypothesis is the model under which the procedure provides no advantage over chance: with two equally probable options, the expected success rate is 50%. The way observations are selected for analysis can invalidate this model.

The entry examines performance-related dropout. Kennedy recommends retaining completed trials even when a participant leaves the experiment, and discusses binomial analysis, hypothetical replacement of missing values, preregistration and sharing excluded data. It provides no new sample, effect estimate or newly preregistered protocol [1].

### Understanding the bias: an example constructed for this bulletin

Imagine two independent trials per person, each with a 50% probability of success. Everyone who fails the first trial drops out; the others continue. If only participants who finish are retained, their first trial is necessarily successful, and their second succeeds on average half the time. The expected rate becomes (1 + 0.5) / 2 = 75%. This value is a mathematical illustration, not an observed result. Selection alone produces an apparent departure from chance here.

### Our analysis: the conditions of the test remain decisive

The binomial test compares a number of successes with what a known probability permits. Its conventional application requires, among other conditions, independent trials, a correctly specified probability and a trial count fixed independently of the results. If sampling stops as soon as a p-value looks favourable, the conventional calculation can become misleading; an appropriate sequential method is needed. A random generator therefore does not, on its own, settle every condition of the analysis.

Likewise, rejecting this statistical model does not identify the cause of the departure. Information leakage, defective randomisation or dependence between trials could also explain it. For reaction times and physiological measurements, repeated observations from the same person require modelling that accounts for their dependence.

The practical contribution is to redirect attention towards the selection process. We recommend specifying stopping and exclusion rules before data collection, then retaining a timestamped log of trials started, completed and removed, together with the reasons. A sensitivity analysis should examine how conclusions change under several plausible missing-data scenarios; replacement under the null hypothesis is not automatically the most adverse scenario.

*Priority full read: consider the example, the conditions of binomial analysis and the transparency rules together; apply the recommendations after checking the experimental design.*

## Consciousness and anomalistics: from experience to testing

Wittmann [2] · Catch-up: published 6 November 2026 · Peer-reviewed article, Hypothesis and Theory category; narrative synthesis and hypotheses

An altered state of consciousness is a marked change in ordinary experience: the perception of the body, time, self or surroundings. Some people report a dissolution of bodily boundaries or a sense of timelessness. Such reports raise a specific psychological question: how can this experience be described and measured? Psi research adds a separate question: is accurate information obtained without a known sensory route?

Wittmann brings together meditation, psychedelics and reduced sensory stimulation. He proposes separately testing the intensity of exceptional experiences and performance on psi tasks. His synthesis also notes null findings. It provides no new sample, preregistered protocol or pooled effect size. It draws, among other sources, on Uzan's [5] distinction between distributed cognition and extrasensory information acquisition [2].

### Our analysis: two success criteria that must remain distinct

An experiment can increase reports of unity or timelessness without improving recognition of a hidden target. An increase in a subjective score and an increase in objective accuracy should therefore constitute two separate hypotheses. A correlation between these scores would still require assessment of common influences: expectations, familiarity with the task, attention or a propensity to report unusual experiences.

The theoretical connection provides testable questions. It does not allow a physical property of nonlocality to be inferred from a feeling of transcending time or space. A philosophical interpretation of consciousness does not replace comparison between competing predictions.

For a future trial, we propose a primary accuracy criterion specified in advance, verifiable targets and masking, and a separate measure of the experienced state. Preregistration should specify questionnaire timing, comparisons and corrections for multiple testing. In a pharmacological study, noticeable drug effects may reveal the assigned group: the quality of blinding should itself be assessed. Finally, replication should address both performance and its proposed relationship with the state of consciousness.

*Targeted reading: introduction, hypotheses H1–H4 and discussion. The value lies in constructing a research programme; earlier experiments cited in the article are not new findings from this week.*

## Metascience: mapping reforms, then evaluating them

Errington et al. [3]; RoRI catalogue [4] · Institutional report, not a journal publication · Catch-up from 3 November; grouped release announced 12 November 2026

Metascience studies research practices themselves. A psi experiment may ask whether someone recognises a hidden target. A metascience study may ask whether preregistration, file sharing or a different way of reviewing articles improves the reliability of research. The effects of these practices then need to be measured, as would those of any intervention.

### What the US report actually studied

On 12 November, the Meta-Moment catalogue announces a grouped release of 18 country studies [4]. The US report, deposited on 3 November as version 3, draws on documents and an earlier corpus of 58 interviews. It distinguishes academic research on scientific practices, quantitative analysis of scientific activity, foundation-funded reforms and federal policies. It maps their actors and objectives, without a new experimental sample or a causal estimate of their effectiveness [3].

In practical terms, this map helps identify who develops methods, who funds their adoption and who sets the rules. It also reveals different objectives: making results more verifiable, changing funding or accelerating discovery. The report describes this organisation. Evaluating the effects of each reform requires further studies.

### A hypothetical example of checking calculations

The following example is constructed for this bulletin; its numbers do not come from the report. Imagine an independent team examining 100 articles: 50 provide the computer program used for the calculations, and 50 do not. Using the available data, the team recovers the main result in 40 articles from the first group and 20 from the second. The proportions are therefore 80% and 40%. The criterion is precise: rerun the calculation on the same data and recover the reported result. This is not a replication using new data.

This comparison shows an association between code availability and successful verification. It does not establish that sharing code causes the entire difference. Teams that share may also have better-documented programs, more resources or simpler analyses. These characteristics could explain part of the difference.

To test causality more effectively, teams could be randomly assigned, before publication, to an offer of help with depositing and documenting code. The groups would then be compared according to their initial assignment, using the same verification protocol. This experiment would estimate the effect of offering help, including when some teams do not use it. The primary criterion, numerical tolerance and rules for handling absent files would be announced before evaluation. The required sample size would depend on the target difference and desired precision.

### Applying this requirement to other reforms

Preregistration means depositing the hypotheses and planned analyses before examining the relevant results. A Registered Report adds assessment of the protocol and a conditional commitment to publication before results are known. These arrangements serve different functions. Their evaluation must specify what they are intended to improve: transparency of changes to analyses, dissemination of null findings or successful replications.

Our analysis. The report provides a map for choosing which arrangements to examine and which actors to approach. National assessments remain reasoned judgements. Since the authors are themselves involved in some of the institutions described, external evaluation of the categories and the effects of reforms would usefully complement this first-hand knowledge.

*Targeted reading: the report's method and institutional categories, followed by the justification of its assessments. The example above explains how to move from an inventory of reforms to a study of their effectiveness; it does not describe an experiment performed by the authors.*

## Synthesis and priority reading

Three levels of evaluation connect: selecting observations, defining what an experiment must demonstrate and checking the arrangements intended to improve research. Their common feature is to make decisions and success criteria open to scrutiny. The practical recommendations in this bulletin are our analyses; they do not constitute additional experimental findings.

2. Wittmann [2]: read the hypotheses and discussion to construct tests distinguishing lived experience from accurate information.
