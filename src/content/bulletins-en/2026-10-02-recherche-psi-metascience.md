This bulletin examines research on possible remote relationships between people, a proposed intentional influence on living organisms, and performance in a word game. It also presents a video-analysis method, two metascience contributions and an academic book. Each study is introduced through its research question, measurements and the conclusions its design can support.

## In brief

**Studies in this issue.** Volume 40(3) of the Journal of Scientific Exploration, published on 29 September 2026, supplies most of the experimental additions. One study asks whether an isolated person's stomach responds to their partner's emotions or meal. Another measures survival in worms exposed to water that the authors propose carries a healing intention. A third examines whether Wordle players perform better after other people have solved the same word.

**Different kinds of findings.** In the gastric experiment, the measure selected in advance does not confirm the expected effect; another, exploratory measure shows differences between groups. The worm study reports increased survival in a mutant strain, with substantial uncertainty about independence of observations and control of bias. Wordle data show a very small change in scores across the day without isolating its cause.

**Examining phenomena and methods.** In anomalistics, a new processing pipeline extracts measurements from videos of luminous phenomena and makes the calculations inspectable. Metascience, the study of research practices and reliability, receives two extended entries: how to establish whether a finding survives different model choices; and how to approve analyses requested by a journal before their results are known.

**Other reading.** Two brief notices address unusual reactions during meditation and a theoretical proposal concerning psychokinesis. An edited volume gathers psychoanalytic contributions on telepathic impressions in clinical encounters. These materials are distinguished from controlled experiments.

**Using the reading guidance.** The label 'full reading recommended' identifies a text whose protocol or argument merits complete examination. For other publications, the guidance names useful parts to consult and explains their relevance. A 'brief notice' flags a contribution without making it a priority. These labels guide reading; they do not rank the validity of the results.

## Psi research

### Testing for a remote gastric response between two people

This study belongs to the psychophysiology of psi: it looks for a relationship between one person's state and another person's physiology when ordinary communication channels are excluded. The receiver therefore does not have to consciously guess an image. The outcome is a bodily signal recorded while their partner performs specified activities.

Electrogastrography, abbreviated EGG, records rhythmic electrical activity of the stomach at the abdominal surface. Amplitude describes signal magnitude; frequency describes its rhythm. This distinction is central because the study seeks to revisit an earlier amplitude finding, whereas the favourable signal occurs in the exploratory frequency analysis.

*Yount, G., Delorme, A., Wahbeh, H., Haro Lopez, J., Taddeo, S., Kriegsman, M., Ahmadzai, S., Tian, R., & Radin, D. (2026). Investigating remote electrogastrogram modulation via emotional and eating stimuli: A sham-controlled pilot study. Journal of Scientific Exploration, 40(3), 352-364.* [DOI 10.31275/20263901](https://doi.org/10.31275/20263901)

**Status.** Peer-reviewed empirical article | 29 September 2026

**Reading guidance.** Full reading recommended, especially the protocol, preregistration and reasons for changing the analysis.

**Question.** Does the gastric electrical activity of an isolated receiver vary when a bonded partner, the sender, views sad images, chooses food, or eats?

**Protocol and sample.** Thirty adult pairs were randomly assigned to a real or sham condition, 15 pairs per group. The receiver underwent electrogastrography while the sender completed the emotional and eating sequence in the real condition. The team and participants were reported as blinded to assignment. Volunteers had to consider telepathy possible. No man served as receiver because of rules concerning electrode placement and body mass index.

**Preregistration.** Procedures and three aims were registered in the KPU registry, entry 1095, before analysis. The criterion derived from the 2005 study concerned EGG amplitude. Frequency analysis was explicitly exploratory. Preregistration timestamps choices made before inspecting results; it does not make a subsequently changed analysis confirmatory. The original circular-shift permutation plan was replaced with a linear mixed model after non-stationarity and non-exchangeability of epochs were identified. The authors state that analyses following this change require exploratory interpretation. Circular shifting would have displaced the time series to construct a chance reference. This requires the shifts to be admissible, an assumption challenged here by systematic changes in the signal over time. The adopted mixed model accounts for repeated measurements within a person.

**Results.** The primary amplitude result was not confirmed: condition x epoch interaction, F(5,168) = 0.69, p = .631. For gastric frequency, the interaction was just below the conventional threshold, F(5,168) = 2.29, p = .048. Exploratory comparisons indicate slowing in the real group during the sad epoch, d = -0.97, FDR-adjusted p = .040, and across the full eating epoch, d = -1.08, FDR-adjusted p = .0374. There was no main effect of condition, p = .451. The interaction tests whether change across epochs differs between groups; the main effect compares groups averaged across epochs. The d value expresses a standardized between-group difference, with a negative sign indicating slowing. FDR adjustment aims to control the expected proportion of false positives among findings declared significant within the corrected family of tests.

**Strengths.** Randomization, sham condition, reported double blinding, accessible preregistration, FDR correction, a repeated-measures model, and transparent disclosure of the analytical change.

**Limitations.** The favourable signal is neither the preregistered outcome nor the planned analysis. Fifteen receivers per group yield unstable effect sizes. Recruitment of people who considered telepathy possible limits generalization, sex roles were imbalanced, and EGG alone cannot identify the proposed autonomic pathway. Multiple epochs, measures and correlations further enlarge the analytical space.

**Actual contribution.** The paper provides a more rigorous sham protocol than the earlier study and a precise candidate for replication. It does not confirm the preregistered hypothesis. A decisive replication should register frequency as the primary endpoint, freeze the mixed model, increase the sample, and add respiration, cardiac activity and skin conductance.

### Testing informed water on worm lifespan

Caenorhabditis elegans is a small worm used as a model organism in biology. Survival and reproduction can be monitored in controlled cultures. A wild-type strain serves as a reference here; a mutant strain with an alteration in the daf-16 gene has a shorter lifespan under the conditions studied.

The authors test whether a healing intention can be retained in a medium and subsequently produce a biological effect without the person who generated it being present. 'Informed water' denotes their preparation hypothesis, not an established physicochemical property of water. R1 and G1 name the two preparations compared with control water.

*Beseme, S., Lins, J., Bengston, W., Nies, M., & McMichael, J. (2026). C. elegans survival analysis: A new path to test storage and scalability of healing intention. Journal of Scientific Exploration, 40(3), 295-302.* [DOI 10.31275/20263877](https://doi.org/10.31275/20263877)

**Status.** Peer-reviewed empirical article | 29 September 2026

**Reading guidance.** Consult the preparation methods, sample-size table and survival curves to assess requirements for independent replication.

**Question.** Do two water preparations produced from healing intention, R1 and G1, alter survival or egg laying in wild-type and short-lived daf-16 nematodes?

**Protocol and sample.** Culture plates received eight drops of R1, G1 or control water. The experiment was neither blinded nor preregistered. The authors report 55 wild-type worms on four plates, followed by six trials in daf-16 mutants. The main table lists 75 worms in each of three conditions, whereas the figure caption describes 75 worms in total. This discrepancy leaves the exact denominator insufficiently clear.

**Results.** No significant effect was observed in wild-type worms. In daf-16, mean lifespan increased from 12.77 days with control water to 15.29 days with G1 and 15.55 days with R1, approximately 20%. Log-rank tests, which compare survival curves across follow-up, yielded p = .002 for G1 and p = .000042 for R1. Egg laying did not differ significantly across groups.

**Strengths.** Established biological model, control from the same water source, two formulations, repetition across six trials, and a null result on a secondary measure.

**Limitations.** Lack of blinding is decisive because daily counting and worm transfers permit observation or handling bias. Treatment is applied at plate and trial level while survival tests appear to count each worm as independent, creating a risk of pseudoreplication. Worms sharing one plate are not necessarily independent replications of the treatment; ignoring this clustering can underestimate uncertainty. The production device is proprietary, individual data and code are not archived, several strains and treatment modes were explored, and William Bengston discloses that he developed the healing method. The sample-size discrepancy further obstructs audit.

**Actual contribution.** The result defines a simple target for external replication. A probative study would require third-party-coded bottles, several genuinely independent batches, randomization at plate level, a hierarchical analysis with plate or trial as the unit, a registered protocol, and deposited individual counts.

### Wordle and the hypothesis of remote collective learning

Wordle is a daily game in which players identify a five-letter word in at most six guesses. Feedback shows which letters occur and which are correctly placed. The number of guesses needed therefore provides a performance measure. WordleBot supplies the aggregate statistics used in this study.

The 'morphic resonance' hypothesis, proposed by Rupert Sheldrake, suggests that similar systems can be influenced by earlier instances without conventional information transmission. Applied here, it leads the authors to look for improvement after other players have found the word. Improvement could also follow changes in the player population or ordinary circulation of the answer: this is the study's causal-identification problem.

*Black, G., Sheldrake, R., & Butzer, B. (2026). Changes in scores throughout the day in Wordle, a word-puzzle played by millions of people: Is morphic resonance at work? Journal of Scientific Exploration, 40(3), 303-314.* [DOI 10.31275/20263953](https://doi.org/10.31275/20263953)

**Status.** Peer-reviewed observational article | 29 September 2026

**Reading guidance.** Consult the construction of WordleBot aggregates, effect sizes and limitations of comparisons between time points.

**Question.** Does aggregate Wordle performance improve as more people have already solved the daily word, as a morphic-resonance account predicts?

**Protocol.** For 30 days, the authors sampled WordleBot at three points separated by about 21.5 hours: 4:00 a.m. in New Zealand, 12:30 p.m. GMT on the following day, and 11:50 p.m. in Hawaii. Reported mean samples were 17,309 players in the morning, 1,792,447 at midday and 1,785,679 in the evening. These are cumulative aggregates generated by a non-public selection algorithm, not independent player-level observations. No preregistration or data deposit is reported.

**Results.** The share solved on the first attempt increased from 0.30% to 0.48%, and the share solved on the second attempt also increased. Shares at attempts four, five and six decreased. P values are often small because the aggregates contain many observations, while every Cohen h effect size is very small, no higher than 0.040. The authors acknowledge many tests without a study-wide adjustment. Cohen's h measures a difference between proportions after statistical transformation; it helps distinguish a change's numerical magnitude from significance alone. Cumulative aggregates reuse some of the same observations, so the snapshots cannot be treated as three independent samples.

**Limitations.** Groups change with time zones, chronobiology, language, recruitment and play habits. Midday and evening samples are cumulative and partly dependent. WordleBot's operation is opaque. Answer circulation, cheating, starting-word strategy and geographic differences are unmeasured. The design therefore generates no prediction that clearly separates morphic resonance, precognition and ordinary factors.

**Actual contribution.** The study reproduces a descriptive pattern found in one of two earlier analyses. It documents a very small time variation that deserves explanation, without identifying its cause. An informative test would require individually tracked players, known time and geography, a controlled target answer, and exclusive predictions registered in advance.

### Two notices from the same issue

#### Unexpected reactions to meditation

*Fernandes, C. A. (2026). Unexpected extemporaneous anomalous reactions (UNEARs) and their relationship with meditation. Journal of Scientific Exploration, 40(3), 315-335.* [DOI 10.31275/20263343](https://doi.org/10.31275/20263343)

**BRIEF NOTICE.** Meditation can be accompanied by unusual bodily sensations or emotional experiences; description alone does not establish their cause. Three of 32 participants from an earlier chronic-back-pain study described physical and emotional reactions in semi-structured diaries during five weeks of meditation. The empirical interest lies in longitudinal recording of adverse events. Three cases, no independent coding and an interpretation based on a subtle body, chakras and nadis cannot establish the proposed etiology.

#### A Responsive Universe hypothesis for macro-PK

*Graboi, D. (2026). Responsive Universe hypothesis for macro-PK: A testable framework linking meaning, measurement, and outcomes. Journal of Scientific Exploration, 40(3), 412-428.* [DOI 10.31275/20263703](https://doi.org/10.31275/20263703)

**BRIEF NOTICE.** Macro-psychokinesis, or macro-PK, denotes the hypothesis of direct mental influence on perceptible physical events, such as deformation of an object. This theoretical essay proposes that the semantic content of a need causally constrains physical outcomes. Its useful component is a set of suggested tests, such as blinded comparisons of different semantic instructions applied to instrumented targets. The presented basis mainly consists of reports, metal-bending demonstrations and unmeasured mechanisms. No new result tests the model.

## Academic anomalistics

### COPP: making luminous-phenomenon videos auditable

Anomalistics examines apparently unusual observations and ways to distinguish competing explanations. A video of an unidentified light first raises a measurement question: which variations belong to the source and which arise from the camera or image processing? Photometry concerns light measurement; with an uncalibrated camera, pixel values do not directly yield an object's physical luminosity.

COPP, the Citizen Orb Photometry Pipeline, proposes a computational procedure whose individual steps can be inspected and repeated. Its immediate purpose is explicit description of the videos. The term 'anomalous' in the title does not itself identify the phenomenon recorded.

*Di Maio, P. (2026). Citizen Orb Photometry Pipeline (COPP): A standardized method for computational analysis of anomalous luminous phenomena from consumer video captures. Journal of Scientific Exploration, 40(3), 336-351.* [DOI 10.31275/20263985](https://doi.org/10.31275/20263985)

**Status.** Peer-reviewed methods article | 29 September 2026

**Reading guidance.** Full reading recommended, alongside the protocol and code to trace the conversion of videos into measurements.

**Method.** COPP is a ten-step Python pipeline that extracts frames, detects the useful region and luminous source, then computes position, apparent size, intensity, red, green and blue (RGB) channels, blue excess, periodicity and trajectory discontinuities. It produces a frame-level CSV table and two dashboards. The protocol is archived on Protocols.io, data and code are deposited on Zenodo, and a web demonstration is available.

**Demonstration.** Three uncalibrated videos recorded in Taiwan in 2021, the United Kingdom in 2011 and Poland in 2025 are analysed. In the Taiwan case, the size-intensity correlation is r = .808 and the size-blue-excess correlation is r = .323. Cycles of 2.4, 7.1 and 8.5 frames are reported. These results describe processed pixels; they do not identify the physical nature of the source.

**Strengths.** Code, data, source videos and protocol are linked to stable identifiers; processing is standardized; adaptive halo analysis avoids direct use of a saturated core; limitations are reported precisely.

**Limitations.** RGB values are transformed by exposure, white balance, gamma and compression. Camera motion is not separated from source motion. Thresholds are heuristic, frame indices sometimes replace timestamps, and neither negative targets nor synthetic videos yet establish false-positive rates. Three selected cases cannot support inference to a shared mechanism or plasma.

**Actual contribution.** COPP v1.0 is primarily an audit infrastructure. Its scientific value will depend on validation against known sources, stabilized cameras, RAW data, synthetic and astronomical controls, and blinded evaluation. This is the kind of open tool anomalistics needs to separate reproducible measurement from object interpretation.

**Resources:** [Zenodo dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/22153927); [COPP v1.0 protocol](https://doi.org/10.17504/protocols.io.n92ldo7eng5b/v1); [demonstration](https://copp-w.vercel.app/)

## Metascience

### Assessing robustness in observational research

In a randomized experiment, random assignment helps make the groups comparable. An observational study analyses situations that researchers did not randomly assign, such as behaviour already recorded. Estimating a relationship then depends strongly on the variables included, exclusions and statistical model.

A 'specification' is a particular set of these choices. Preregistering one specification fixes it before analysis and limits some opportunistic adjustments without ensuring that the chosen model adequately describes the situation. The authors therefore discuss how to organize several plausible analyses so that their influence on conclusions becomes visible.

*Ben-Menachem, J., Galper, A., & Fishman, N. (2026). A replication framework for observational research: From specification restriction to specification expansion. Sociological Methods & Research. Online first.* [DOI 10.1177/00491241261490850](https://doi.org/10.1177/00491241261490850)

**Status.** Peer-reviewed theoretical article | 29 September 2026

**Reading guidance.** Full reading recommended for the argument about observational research and the requirements of robustness analysis.

**Thesis.** Restrictive reforms, including one analysis plan fixed in advance, are effective for controlling false positives when the probability assumptions of a randomized experiment are credible. In observational work, a preregistered model can merely freeze a fragile or misspecified choice. The authors propose expansive reforms: analysis multiverses, specification curves, confirmatory replications and meta-analysis. A multiverse computes results across an explicit set of admissible analytical choices. A specification curve displays how estimates change with those choices. The aim is to examine stability while also reporting analyses that weaken the result.

**Why it matters here.** The Wordle study illustrates the problem. One time contrast removes neither geographic change in the sample, cumulative data, nor metric choices. Researchers must show how the conclusion changes across plausible definitions and models, then compare the pattern with independent data.

**Strength.** The restriction-expansion distinction avoids treating preregistration as a universal remedy. It places replication within a cumulative architecture in which robustness depends on variation across assumptions and samples.

**Limitation.** The article is argumentative and does not estimate comparative reform effectiveness. A multiverse also increases multiplicity; researchers must define the model universe, publish every result, weight plausibility and avoid selecting the favourable specification after inspection.

### Registered Revisions: precommitting reviewer-requested reanalysis

Journal peer review can lead reviewers to request additional analyses. These requests usually occur after the initial findings are known. They create another set of choices: which exclusions to apply, which subgroups to examine, and which analyses to include in the final article?

Registered Reports are a publishing format in which a protocol is reviewed and can receive in-principle acceptance before results are known. Registered Revisions apply this logic when a manuscript is revised: the commitment concerns additional analyses to be performed. This report describes the development and pilot of an evaluation of that editorial policy.

*Haber, N., Banks, G. C., Bowman, N., Carriere, K., Impellizzeri, F., Fellnhofer, K., van Ravenzwaaij, D., Wennberg, K., Whaley, P., Blersch, A., Wiley, S., Daley, M., Lueck, J., Errington, T. M., & Nosek, B. A. (2026). Registered Revisions Meta Trial Pilot Report. MetaArXiv, version 1.* [DOI 10.31222/osf.io/j64ct_v1](https://doi.org/10.31222/osf.io/j64ct_v1)

**Status.** Preprint and technical report, not peer reviewed | 8 September 2026; highlighted 29 September

**Reading guidance.** Consult the revision procedure, materials for journals and pilot difficulties; the policy's effects have not yet been estimated.

**Innovation.** When a revision decision requests new data or analyses, authors first submit a plan. The editor and reviewers assess that plan before new results are known. Acceptance then depends on compliant execution, not favourable results. The policy transfers the central principle of Registered Reports to the revision stage.

**Infrastructure.** The Pathfinder pilot involved seven journals in co-designing templates, consent, editorial tracking and an application independent of submission platforms. The project replaces one trial with several journal trials connected through shared infrastructure, a study-in-a-kit, and prospective meta-analysis. Proposed primary outcomes concern time and editorial process rather than the share of significant results.

**Strengths.** The policy addresses a real blind spot: analysis added after review can become a new source of selection and HARKing, meaning that a hypothesis constructed after seeing results is presented as though it had been formulated beforehand. The multi-journal design combines central standardization with local adaptation and documents implementation failures.

**Limitations.** This is a development and feasibility report, not a meta-trial result. No effect on quality, acceptance, delay or publication bias is yet estimated. Consent, editorial burden, software bugs and journal heterogeneity may affect recruitment and comparability.

**Psi application.** A journal could use this policy when a reviewer asks for a new exclusion, transformation or subgroup after seeing the data. The plan would be archived and approved before calculation, making the boundary between verification and exploration much clearer.

## New university press book

### Telepathy in the psychoanalytic clinical process

In psychoanalytic terminology, transference refers to feelings and expectations directed by a patient towards the analyst in relation to their relational history; countertransference refers to the analyst's reactions within that relationship. The volume examines impressions of unexplained knowledge or coincidence that clinicians have interpreted as telepathic. This notice concerns the scope and contents described by the publisher; it is not a full review of the chapters.

*Reichbart, R. (Ed.). (2026). Telepathy in the Clinical Process: A Century of Psychoanalytic Mystery. Routledge. 222 pp.* [publisher record](https://www.routledge.com/9781041006589)

**Status.** Academic edited volume | 29 September 2026

**Reading guidance.** Consult the contents and chapters relevant to clinical history or psychoanalysis; this notice is based on the publisher's description.

The volume gathers historical and clinical contributions on coincidences and telepathic impressions reported in transference and countertransference. It documents how different psychoanalytic schools address these episodes. The materials are clinical vignettes and conceptual analyses, not controlled experimental tests. Its primary value is historiographic and clinical: it concerns narrative status, interpretation and therapeutic effect, not an estimate of the existence or frequency of telepathy.

## Synthesis

The new issue of the Journal of Scientific Exploration presents three research approaches with distinct challenges. The EGG pilot has a useful experimental architecture, while its preregistered result is null and the favourable signal follows an analytical change. The C. elegans study reports a numerically strong biological effect whose lack of blinding, experimental unit and documentation block causal interpretation. The Wordle study reproduces a very small aggregate regularity without separating competing explanations.

Two methodological advances are more robust. COPP makes image transformations inspectable and shares data, code and protocol, even though physical validation remains unfinished. In metascience, specification expansion shows why preregistering a single model is insufficient for observational data; Registered Revisions target another point of flexibility, analyses requested after submission.

### Priority reading

1. Yount et al., to separate the null preregistered endpoint from the exploratory signal and to design a confirmatory replication of gastric frequency.

2. Ben-Menachem et al., for a framework directly applicable to observational psi research and analyses with many specifications.

3. Di Maio, to audit a concrete open tool in anomalistics from source video to output metrics.
