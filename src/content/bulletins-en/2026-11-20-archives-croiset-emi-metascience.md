Three articles published online from 17 to 19 November provide new material: archives concerning Gerard Croiset's involvement in the Moro case, a Danish survey of near-death experiences and an empirical audit of scientific file sharing. The metascience section continues the previous issue's topic, this time presenting observed data. The OSF notice is limited to the milestone of 16 November.

## Police clairvoyance and the Moro case archives

Wittmann [1] · First online publication 18 November 2026 · Historical journal article, advance publication for volume 27 of 2027 · No new experimental test

Police clairvoyance involves proposing information that might assist an investigation without an identified ordinary source. Assessing it requires preserving precise statements before their correspondence with events becomes known. A retrospective account raises a different problem: what information was already circulating, which elements were retained and which failed?

Wittmann re-examines Gerard Croiset's archives: a recording, drawings and photographs. He distinguishes Croiset's operationally unsuccessful indications from the collective séance of 2 April 1978 that produced the name Gradoli. Via Gradoli housed a leader of the kidnappers, while Moro was held elsewhere. No experimental sample, preregistration or statistical effect is presented [1].

### Our analysis of the value of the archives

The documentary gain lies in being able to compare a preserved statement with what actually happened. A failure to locate someone belongs in the assessment alongside a matching name. Success must be defined: identifying an actionable location before its discovery differs from establishing a connection afterwards.

Source independence remains essential. Several accounts may derive from the same witness and then constitute only one documentary chain. A feeling of involuntary movement during a séance cannot, by itself, rule out the transmission of knowledge already present within the group. Conversely, considering that explanation does not establish that a participant actually possessed this knowledge.

The article's comparison with a lottery requires qualification: words produced by a group are not sequences of independent, equally probable letters. Estimating the rarity of a match would require defining admissible names, competing responses and matching criteria in advance. Without that model, no precise chance probability is justified. The article therefore enriches a historical dossier; it does not turn the coincidence into a controlled test.

*Priority full read: examine Croiset's documents, unsuccessful location attempts and competing explanations of the séance together.*

## Near death experiences in Denmark

Stripp [2–3] · Published 19 November 2026 · Peer-reviewed observational study · Hypotheses supplied in an appendix; prospective analytic preregistration not documented

A near-death experience, or NDE, here denotes a set of unusual memories reported after a life-threatening situation. Classification relies on a questionnaire; it does not determine the exact physiological time when the experience formed. The survey asks about its frequency among respondents and its associations with their existential lives.

Of 2,262 survivors of out-of-hospital cardiac arrests occurring from 2016 to 2020, 855 respond and 738 are analysed. The 20-item Near-Death Experience Content (NDE-C) questionnaire classifies 45 people at the threshold of 27 points: 6.1% of those analysed. Data collection took place in 2021. The other 117 respondents are excluded because they did not answer the memory question [2–3].

### Results and interpretation of the associations

The association with spiritual needs corresponds to a relative risk ratio (RRR) of 2.51, with a 95% interval of 1.33–4.73. For meaning in life, the RRR is 0.72 [0.51; 1.01], p = 0.054: the observed trend does not cross the 0.05 threshold. Exploratory analyses of distressing content identify no significant association with the outcomes examined [2–3].

The model compares the relative probabilities of belonging to memory categories as a function of the variables examined. An RRR above 1 indicates an association with the NDE category relative to the reference category without memories. It does not mean that the NDE subsequently multiplied a person's needs. Since all these measures were collected together, their causal ordering remains undetermined.

### Our analysis of strengths and limitations

Registry-based recruitment defines the invited population, improving interpretation of the denominator compared with a call for personal accounts. However, the 6.1% rate describes retained respondents, not automatically all Danish survivors. Nonresponse and exclusion on the memory item may alter the group's composition. Administrative or ethical registration is not equivalent to preregistration of analyses.

The absence of a significant association for distressing experiences does not demonstrate their harmlessness: small subgroups may leave considerable uncertainty. Likewise, attributing a lower frequency to cultural context would require a comparison with harmonised recruitment, questionnaires and time intervals. This study provides a bounded clinical description, without a test of concealed veridical perception or direct measurement of anomalous information acquisition.

*Priority full read: read the article with its supplements, checking exclusions, the direction of the regressions and the distinction between a statistical trend and a significant result.*

## Metascience and access to data and code

Kim and Bajare [4] · Published 17 November 2026 · Peer-reviewed accepted article, advance version · Documentary audit; preregistration not reported

The 13 November issue illustrated the role of code sharing with a hypothetical example. This week, Kim and Bajare provide an actual audit: they check which files a reader can locate through articles and their links. The corpus concerns machine-learning models predicting concrete properties, without a psi experiment [4].

### What the 419 articles actually provide

Complete data are provided in 90 of 419 articles, or 21.5%. In 211 articles, or 50.4%, only the data sources are cited: the dataset actually assembled to train the model is not presented. The other 118 articles do not share their data [4].

For programs, the categories are distributed as follows: 380 articles without code, 20 with source code, 17 with only pseudocode and 2 with a link that has become inaccessible. The rates of 90.7% without code and 4.8% with source code are therefore not complementary. Pseudocode describes a procedure without necessarily providing an executable program [4].

### How classification is checked

A second assessor independently reclassifies 84 articles. Kappas reach 0.942 for data and 0.880 for code [4]. This check concerns agreement in classification. It certifies neither data quality nor predictive accuracy. Preregistration of the audit is not reported.

Our analysis. The contribution is to measure what can actually be obtained, beyond an availability statement. The category of sources merely cited is particularly useful: knowing where data originated does not automatically allow reconstruction of the retained rows, exclusions and transformations.

The result remains a rate of access to resources. The authors do not rerun the models and therefore do not estimate a rate of successful reproduction. The corpus is limited to a specialty and one bibliographic database. For psi research, the checking method is transferable, whereas the percentages would require an audit specific to the field.

*Targeted reading: sharing categories, independent checking and limitations. The novelty of this notice lies in the observed frequencies and how they are checked.*

## Synthesis and priority reading

The Moro dossier makes visible the difference between a retrospective match and an actionable location. The Danish survey separates the frequency of accounts from their psychological associations without physiologically dating the experiences. The computational audit reminds readers that possessing a reference, obtaining files and recovering the calculation are three different operations. The OSF change gives this traceability requirement an immediate application.

1. Wittmann [1]: the full text for the documentary chain and unsuccessful indications.

2. Stripp [2–3]: article and supplements for recruitment, exclusions and positive or nonsignificant findings.

3. Kim and Bajare [4]: method and limitations to distinguish access to resources from reproduction of calculations.
