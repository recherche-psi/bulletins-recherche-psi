Yes: the brain can prepare for an event before it happens. When you are waiting for a sound, your brain activity may already reflect that expectation.

Isabelle Hoxha and colleagues studied this phenomenon in 42 participants, who were asked to identify images or sounds. In some trials, a cue correctly predicted the type of stimulus 80% of the time.

The researchers recorded electrical brain activity before the stimulus appeared. In trials with a cue, a computer program distinguished anticipation of an image from anticipation of a sound with an average accuracy of 66%.

The program was therefore recognising the brain's preparation. This finding did not show that participants knew about an unpredictable future event.

To investigate presentiment, researchers would need to go further: record brain activity, then randomly select the target only after that measurement, while preventing any information leakage.

**Predicting from clues and anticipating an as-yet unknown target are two different questions.** This study provides a tool for distinguishing them more precisely: identifying what the brain expects, trial by trial.
