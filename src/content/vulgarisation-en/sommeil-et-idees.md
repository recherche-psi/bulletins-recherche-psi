You get stuck on a problem, take a break and an idea comes to you later. What role might sleep play in that process?

Thomas Bieth and colleagues asked participants to think about a puzzle before and after a 90-minute break. Some stayed awake. Others slept, with or without entering REM sleep.

The researchers examined associations between ideas related to the problem. After REM sleep, concepts that were initially distant became more closely associated, while some strong associations that were unhelpful for solving the problem weakened.

You can imagine a network of paths between ideas: after sleep, some routes become easier to reach. This is an analogy to help explain the finding, not a literal description of the brain.

However, participants were no more successful at solving the puzzle in this study. Changing the available associations therefore does not guarantee the right answer.

**Sleep can reorganise how a problem is represented without automatically delivering its solution.** This distinction helps researchers study the emergence of new ideas: they need to measure both changes in thinking and actual success.
